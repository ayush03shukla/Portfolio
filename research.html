Research Areas

I am broadly interested in applied electromagnetics, especially where systems, sensing, and signal processing come together for real-world engineering problems.

Radar and mmWave Sensing

My current work is in radar-based sensing, especially using 77 GHz FMCW radar for detecting small, low-reflectivity objects in cluttered environments. This has introduced me to the full radar processing chain, from data collection and calibration to range-angle processing and target detection.

  • UWB antenna design
  • Wearable / WBAN antennas
  • Full-wave EM simulation
  • Radiation and bending analysis

Signal Processing for Detection

I am interested in how signal processing can make weak targets visible in noisy or cluttered measurements. In my current work, I have explored background subtraction, clutter suppression, sparse reconstruction, and detection methods for radar data.

EMC/EMI and Surrogate Modelling

My earlier research focused on electromagnetic compatibility problems, particularly estimating EMI filter insertion loss. I explored how surrogate models can reduce simulation cost while still capturing important frequency behaviour in electromagnetic systems.

Antennas and RF Systems

I have also worked on antenna design for wireless body-area network applications. This helped me understand the hardware side of RF systems, including antenna geometry, radiation behaviour, bending effects, and simulation-based design.

Learning-Based Extensions

More recently, I have started exploring how learning-based methods can be connected with model-based radar algorithms. I am especially interested in approaches that improve performance while still remaining interpretable and physically meaningful.
